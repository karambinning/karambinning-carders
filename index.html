Front of House

A neighbourhood shop on London Road.

By the proprietors, P&J Carder

Carder’s has stood at number one-twenty-eight London Road for years — a small family-run shop that keeps South Lynn ticking. People come in for the paper. They come back for the chat.

It is the sort of place where the regulars are known by name and the new faces are remembered by the second visit. A corner shop in the old, genuine sense of the phrase — useful, friendly, open when you need it to be.

Inside, the racks are stocked with the morning’s nationals and locals, magazines for every interest, a careful off-licence shelf, and the everyday essentials that you only realise you’ve run out of at half past nine on a Tuesday evening.

There is a National Lottery terminal at the counter, top-ups for the major mobile networks, greetings cards for last-minute occasions, and the kind of steady, unfussy service that has kept neighbourhood shops at the centre of British high streets for the better part of a century.

A proper corner shop — the paper in the morning, a bottle of something in the evening, and a familiar hello in between.
